The target workload ran successfully

Each of four requests completed 130,560 input tokens plus 512 output tokens = 131,072. The run used precision-LRU 160, cooperative expert compute, no MTP, an 11,970,048,000-byte expert bank and 8 GiB FP8 KV. Prefix caching was disabled; seed was 42 and temperature zero. The four prompts repeated public serving code/documentation with distinct tasks; they are a synthetic workload proxy. Prompt sources and hashes · launcher · exact workload.

The trace observed 319 actual four-request decode steps, with one decode row per request and computed contexts between 130,560 and 131,070. The common steady window lasted 11.6968 seconds and yielded 108.75 aggregate tok/s, or 27.19 tok/s per request. Total generation, including all four prefills, took 504.02 seconds. Average expert traffic was 398.61 MiB per steady step, or 99.65 MiB per output token; the unique-expert hit fraction was 84.42%. Steady timing · summary · outputs.

These rates describe one instrumented diagnostic baseline, not unprofiled performance or a confidence estimate. Tracing added one small kernel per cached layer and a 7.875 MiB buffer. The first startup failed before generation because callable RPC serialization was rejected. The single permitted correction used named Worker RPC methods; the next run completed successfully. K3 shifts move 10% or 20% of the total resident-byte budget toward or away from K3, with K2 receiving the remainder. Every split worsened held-out traffic by 2.91–24.40%. Delayed admission saved 2.19% held-out but cost 11.07% on selection; it cannot be chosen retrospectively from the held-out result. One-forward protection was effectively unchanged held-out.

Decision: stop at the offline gate. The preregistered requirement was at least 20% fewer held-out transfer bytes before any live candidate comparison. No candidate qualified, so no full-model candidate, parity trial or deployment change was warranted. Keep precision-LRU 160 and its current admission controls. Preregistered gates and stop criteria.

Verdict: refuted within the tested fixed-budget candidates. This does not prove cache allocation can never improve. Candidate results are frozen-routing byte counts, not measured throughput; live routing could change. The workload is synthetic rather than the user’s corpus, and 512 outputs do not establish 32K-output endurance. Larger cache budgets and other optimization families were not tested.
